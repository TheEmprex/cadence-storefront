Standards

Four commitments, all of them checkable.

A standard you cannot verify is a slogan. Everything on this page can be checked against the label, the certificate, or the research — which is the only reason it is worth printing.

01 — Every active, at its amount

No proprietary blends. Ever.

A proprietary blend is a label that gives you a total rather than a breakdown: "Calm Complex — 800 mg" covering six ingredients. It is legal, it is common, and it exists for exactly one reason, which is that it lets a manufacturer put a famous ingredient on the front of the box and almost none of it in the box. If a blend contains one ingredient at 780 mg and five at 4 mg each, the label reads identically to one that is evenly split. You cannot tell. That is the point of it.

Every Cadence label lists every active with its own number next to it. You can compare those numbers to the research without having to trust us, which is a better arrangement for both of us.

02 — Tested every batch, published every batch

Heavy metals — lead, cadmium, arsenic and mercury — screened by an independent US laboratory on every production lot. Not on the first lot, not annually, not on a sample the manufacturer chose. Every lot.

The certificate is published on this site next to its batch number, so the lot code on the bottom of your box can be matched to the paperwork for the thing you are actually holding. Botanicals and minerals draw metals out of the soil they grow in; that is not a scandal, it is agriculture. What matters is whether anyone is measuring, and whether they will show you.

03 — Made in an FDA-registered, cGMP facility

Manufactured under current Good Manufacturing Practice in an FDA-registered facility, with identity testing on incoming raw materials and finished-product stability data on file. Creatine additionally carries our own third-party impurity assay — creatinine, dicyandiamide and dihydrotriazine — because it is the most counterfeited raw material in this industry and a certificate with our lot number on it is worth more than a licence to print somebody else's logo.

Probiotic counts are guaranteed through end of shelf life, not at time of manufacture. This is the single most avoided number on a probiotic label. Bacteria die off in storage, so a count measured on the day it was made can be a fraction of that by the time it reaches your cupboard. Where a competitor prints a very large figure without saying when it was measured, that is usually why.

04 — Honest about timing

We will tell you before you buy how long something takes, including when the answer is unhelpful. Build does nothing for three to four weeks. Ease needs a cycle or two to judge. Intimate is gradual and undramatic. Rest works the first night, which is the only genuinely satisfying answer in the range.

The commercial logic of saying this out loud is not complicated: people who know what to expect do not quit in week two and conclude they were sold nothing.

The other list

What we deliberately leave out.

Formulation is mostly refusal. These are the ingredients that had an obvious case for inclusion and did not make it, with the reason attached.

Not in the rangeWhy not
Chasteberry (vitex) The obvious choice for a luteal formula, and routinely flagged for interaction with hormonal contraception — which a large share of the people this is for are taking. We used saffron instead. It costs more and it keeps everybody in.
Melatonin Not because it fails to work. Because it is typically sold at many times the useful amount, and the next-morning fog people describe is usually the melatonin rather than the sleep.
Senna, cascara, other laxatives A fast visible result and a slow invisible problem. Nothing here works by moving your bowels.
Diuretic botanicals Pushing water out is not the same as helping you hold the right amount of it, and the framing drags a period product into weight-loss territory it has no business being in.
CBD Not a brand preference — it is not lawful as a dietary supplement ingredient.
5-HTP Interaction risk with a class of medication a meaningful share of this audience takes.
Kava The liver signal is not worth the sleep benefit when glycine and theanine are available.
Valerian Smells like a compost bin, tastes worse, and would ruin a drink people are meant to look forward to.
Mega-dose biotin It interferes with common lab assays, including thyroid and cardiac tests, at the doses beauty supplements like to print on the front.
Calcium in Ease A reasonable case, a worse dose. It would take four capsules a day to deliver a meaningful amount of a mineral most people get through food.
Artificial colours, sucralose No functional argument for either, and this audience reads labels.
Caffeine Most people reading this are well supplied. Leaving it out means Build can be taken at any hour.

How we talk about what these do

Supplements in the United States are regulated as food, not as medicine. That means we can describe how an ingredient supports a normal body function, and we cannot say it treats, prevents or cures anything — because if it did, it would be a drug, and it would need a completely different kind of evidence and approval behind it.

In practice this is a line we hold on purpose rather than reluctantly. You will see us name symptoms — bloating, a shorter fuse, sleep that will not land, discomfort in the first few days — because those are real and describing them accurately is useful. You will not see us name the conditions those symptoms sometimes belong to, and you will not see us promise to fix them. Anyone who does is either not reading the rules or is betting you will not check.

If you have a symptom that needs diagnosing, that is a doctor's job. This is a supplement company.
