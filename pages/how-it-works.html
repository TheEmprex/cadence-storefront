How it works

A month isn't one thing.

Most supplement ranges are organised around ingredients — a magnesium product, a collagen product, a greens product — and then leave you to work out when any of it matters. Cadence is organised around when your body is actually asking for something.

The architecture

There is one daily base and there are phase formulas. The base does not move: creatine works by saturation, so it is taken every day regardless of where you are in your cycle, and skipping a week costs you more than a hard session gains. Everything else comes in when it is needed and stops when it is not.

That sounds like a merchandising idea. It is mostly a dosing one. Taking a luteal formula for twelve days a month instead of thirty is cheaper, obviously — but it also lets us put vitamin B6 in it at a dose that does something, without the accumulation that comes from taking that amount every day of every month for years. The shape of the range is what makes the formula safe.

The four phases

Two of them come with complaints. Two of them do not, and we are not going to invent a product for the weeks you feel fine.

You will notice there is nothing sold specifically for the follicular and ovulation stretch. That is deliberate. It is the part of the month most people report feeling good, and a supplement sold into a phase with no felt problem is a supplement sold on hope.

Sized to one cycle

Every box we make contains what a single cycle uses. Thirty servings of the daily base. Twenty-four capsules of the luteal formula, because that is two a day for twelve days. Five sticks of the period formula, because that is what a period is for most people.

This is why a Flow box looks small next to a tub of thirty scoops. The alternative — the industry default — is to sell you six months of something in one container, bill you monthly for it, and let you discover the mismatch when the third unopened box arrives. Sizing to the cycle means one delivery, one renewal date, and nothing left over.

If your cycle is irregular, or absent

Build, Rest and Intimate have nothing to do with the calendar and work exactly the same either way. Ease and Flow are built around the luteal and menstrual phases, so take them by how you feel rather than by a date — the day numbers on this site are a shorthand for describing the pattern, not a schedule you have to hit. If those phases do not apply to you at all, build a kit without them.

The subscription

It renews every 30 days, flat. Not on a predicted cycle length — cycles run anywhere from 21 to 35 days and vary month to month, and a delivery schedule that tries to track that produces missed boxes and support tickets rather than better timing. The box is sized generously enough to absorb the drift.

You can skip a delivery, move the date, change what is in it or cancel from your account in a couple of clicks. No phone call, no retention script. There is no free trial that quietly converts, because that structure is the reason this category has the reputation it has.
