What a pass actually means

Every lot is screened against the strictest limit that applies to it, which in practice is usually California's Proposition 65 threshold for lead rather than a federal supplement limit — Prop 65 is considerably tighter than the general standard, and a formula that clears it clears everything else. A pass means the measured amount sits under that threshold, not that the result was zero.

Zero is not a realistic target and anyone promising it is selling you something. Plants take up trace metals from soil, and minerals are mined from ground that contains other things. Every green vegetable you have ever eaten carries a measurable amount of arsenic. The question worth asking is not whether a number exists but whether anyone measured it, against what limit, and whether they will show you the paper.

The creatine assay, which is separate

Creatine monohydrate carries a specific risk that heavy-metal screening does not cover: the manufacturing process can leave behind creatinine, dicyandiamide and dihydrotriazine, and it is the most counterfeited raw material in sports nutrition. So Build gets its own third-party assay for those three, on top of the standard panel.

We considered licensing a branded creatine instead. It costs roughly double, and because the counterfeit problem attaches to the brand itself, buying the logo would have meant paying the premium and then verifying it independently anyway. Testing our own and publishing the result was cheaper and produces a document with our lot number on it, which is the more useful object.

Can't find your lot?

Certificates go up within ten business days of a lot being released, so a very recent box may arrive slightly ahead of its paperwork. If your code is not listed, send it to us with your order number and we will send the certificate directly.
